The Elder Scrolls: Blades (2019)

Bethesda's attempt to bring Elder Scrolls to phones, in bite-sized form.

Elder Scrolls, condensed for phones

The Elder Scrolls: Blades released in 2019 as Bethesda's answer to the mobile market, built around short dungeon-crawling sessions rather than the sprawling exploration the console games are known for. The framing device is simple: your home town has been destroyed, and rebuilding it becomes the game's central, ongoing hook while you clear dungeons and take on quests to fund the reconstruction.

What it does differently

Blades trades open-world wandering for procedurally structured dungeon runs, designed to be finished in a few minutes at a time. Combat is simplified accordingly, built around touch controls rather than the first-person freedom of the mainline games. It's a reasonable adaptation of the formula for the platform, even if it inevitably feels much smaller in scope than anything else on this list.

Where it stands now

Blades never became the flagship mobile hit Bethesda may have hoped for, and it's received far less active content support in recent years compared to when it launched. It's still available and playable, but treat it as a curiosity rather than an essential entry if you're working through the series.

Should you play it today?

If you already love Elder Scrolls and want something low-commitment for short sessions, it's worth a look, and it's free to download. Just don't go in expecting anything close to the depth of the console and PC entries.
